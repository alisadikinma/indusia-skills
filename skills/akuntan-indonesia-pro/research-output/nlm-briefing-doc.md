# Strategic Transition to SAK EP: Financial Reporting Impacts and Taxation Implications

## Executive Summary

The landscape of financial reporting for private entities in Indonesia is undergoing a fundamental shift with the transition from SAK ETAP (Accounting Standards for Entities Without Public Accountability) to SAK EP (Accounting Standards for Private Entities). Effective January 1, 2025, with early adoption permitted since 2022, SAK EP aligns more closely with international standards (IFRS for SMEs). This transition introduces more complex recognition, measurement, and disclosure requirements, particularly concerning expected credit losses (ECL), revenue recognition via a five-step model, and mandatory recognition of post-employment benefits.

Analysis of real-world cases indicates that this transition significantly impacts the valuation of assets, liabilities, and equity, often resulting in a more conservative financial position. Furthermore, the divergence between SAK EP accounting treatments and Indonesian tax regulations (DJP) necessitates rigorous fiscal reconciliation and the recognition of deferred tax. Concurrently, the implementation of the "Coretax" era via PER-11/PJ/2025 introduces new administrative requirements, such as extended deadlines for e-Faktur and new withholding obligations for individual business owners.

## Detailed Analysis of Key Themes

### 1. The Shift from SAK ETAP to SAK EP
The transition represents a move from a simplified accounting framework to one that prioritizes a "fair value" and "accrual-based" approach. SAK EP addresses the limitations of SAK ETAP, which was often viewed as too simple for entities requiring more dynamic financial policies.

#### Key Accounting Adjustments
| Feature | SAK ETAP | SAK EP |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Financial Instruments** | Measured at nominal value. | Measured at amortized cost using the Effective Interest Rate (EIR). |
| **Receivables** | Bad debt recognized only when clearly uncollectible. | Expected Credit Loss (ECL) model; requires aging analysis and historical loss estimation. |
| **Revenue** | Often based on general completion estimates. | Five-step model: Identify contract, identify performance obligations, determine price, allocate price, recognize upon fulfillment. |
| **Post-Employment Benefits** | Recognition was optional. | Mandatory recognition and systematic measurement (actuarial or simplified approach). |
| **Investment in Associates** | Historical cost method. | Equity method (for significant influence). |

### 2. Taxation and Fiscal Reconciliation Implications
While SAK EP improves reporting quality, it creates wider gaps with Indonesian tax law (UU PPh). Tax calculations remain bound by fiscal rules, leading to permanent and temporary differences.

*   **Bad Debt Provisions:** Under SAK EP, ECL reduces net income. However, tax law only allows deductions for debts that are truly uncollectible and meet specific legal criteria (Pasal 6 ayat 1 huruf h UU PPh). This requires a **positive fiscal reconciliation**.
*   **Post-Employment Benefits:** SAK EP requires estimating and recording future liabilities. Tax-wise, these are only deductible when actually paid or transferred to an approved pension fund.
*   **Asset Impairment:** SAK EP recognizes losses when an asset's carrying amount exceeds its recoverable amount. Tax law does not recognize these estimated losses, requiring reconciliation.
*   **Revenue Timing:** SAK EP might defer revenue if performance obligations are not met. If a tax invoice (Faktur Pajak) has been issued, the revenue may still be taxable fiscally, creating a temporary difference.

### 3. Regulatory Updates: The Coretax Era (PER-11/PJ/2025)
The implementation of the Coretax system introduces several administrative changes aimed at modernizing tax compliance.

*   **Extended E-Faktur Deadline:** The deadline for uploading e-Faktur is extended from the 15th to the **20th of the following month**.
*   **Individual Business Owner Obligations:** Freelancers and UMKM owners (Orang Pribadi) are now mandatory withholders for rental transactions.
    *   **PPh 23:** 2% for rentals of equipment/non-land assets.
    *   **PPh Final Pasal 4(2):** 10% for rentals of land and buildings.
*   **SPT Simplification:** SPT Masa PPh is consolidated into two types: PPh Pasal 21/26 (employees) and PPh Unifikasi (PPh 22, 23, 15, and 4(2)).

### 4. Financial Reporting for Manufacturing Entities
Manufacturing reporting is inherently more complex than trading, as it involves the transition from raw materials to Work in Progress (WIP) and finally finished goods.

*   **Components of HPP (COGS):** Includes Direct Raw Materials, Direct Labor, and Factory Overhead (utilities, factory rent, machine depreciation).
*   **Inventory Categories:** Must distinguish between Raw Materials, WIP, and Finished Goods.
*   **Markup Strategies:** Some entities add a "production profit" margin when transferring goods from the factory to the warehouse to show a more realistic trading profit, though this requires specific accounting entries to ensure net profit remains accurate.

### 5. Corporate Risk and Internal Control: Fraud Analysis
Several cases highlight the critical need for internal controls and the dangers of external societal factors like online gambling (judol).

*   **Case 1: PT Asta International (Sinjai Branch):** A cashier (Putri Ayu Lestari Ilham) embezzled **Rp730 million** from motorcycle sales and workshop payments between October and December 2024. The fraud was detected when a Finance Operational Supervisor noticed the cash-on-hand report was growing without being deposited into the company account. The funds were used for online gambling.
*   **Case 2: PT Cipta Niaga Semesta (CNS):** A cashier (Tia Agustina) embezzled **Rp90 million** by manipulating bank collection deposits. While the audit showed a required deposit of ~Rp394 million, only ~Rp304 million was deposited. The funds were used for online gambling and personal use.
*   **Common Denominator:** Both cases involved cashiers with direct access to physical cash and reporting, highlighting a failure in the segregation of duties and frequent auditing.

## Actionable Insights

### 1. Strategic Accounting Readiness
*   **Perform Gap Analysis:** Entities currently using SAK ETAP should conduct a comprehensive review of their accounts—specifically receivables, revenue contracts, and employee benefits—to quantify the impact of SAK EP before the 2025 deadline.
*   **Implement ECL Models:** Move away from the simple write-off method. Develop an aging schedule for all receivables and calculate loss percentages based on historical data to satisfy SAK EP requirements.
*   **Adopt Multi-Step Reporting:** For manufacturing and complex private entities, utilize multi-step P&L formats to provide better insights into gross vs. operating margins.

### 2. Tax Compliance and Reconciliation
*   **Prepare for Deferred Tax:** Since SAK EP introduces temporary differences (e.g., benefit estimations, asset impairments), entities must begin recording deferred tax assets or liabilities as per SAK EP Chapter 29.
*   **Update Withholding Procedures:** Individual business owners must update their internal systems to accommodate the new requirement to withhold and report tax on rents under the Coretax system.
*   **Monitor E-Faktur Deadlines:** Adjust administrative workflows to ensure all e-Faktur are uploaded by the 20th of the following month to avoid penalties.

### 3. Risk Mitigation and Internal Controls
*   **Segregate Duties:** Ensure that the individual handling cash collections is not the same individual responsible for bank reconciliations or financial reporting.
*   **Mandatory Audits:** Implement frequent, unannounced cash counts (sidak) for branches or departments handling high volumes of cash, as demonstrated in the Sinjai embezzlement case.
*   **Employee Awareness:** Provide education on the dangers of online gambling and maintain strict oversight of company digital and physical assets.

### 4. Digitalization and Software Integration
*   **Transition to Integrated ERP:** Manual recording is highly prone to human error and fraud. Implementing software that connects production (WIP), inventory, and finance (EIR calculations) can automate SAK EP compliance and provide real-time monitoring of cash flows.
*   **Utilize E-Bupot Tools:** Use digital platforms to manage "Unifikasi" reporting requirements to ensure that multiple types of PPh are filed accurately in one system.
