## Standard working sequence (full business-model brief)

```
1. DIAGNOSE   → Where is value created in the chain? Who realizes it? (02 §value-metric)
2. IDEATE     → Generate 6–10 candidate models, force ≥3 non-obvious (03)
3. MATCH      → Map candidates onto named patterns; borrow proven mechanics (01)
4. PRICE      → Choose value metric + mechanism; check against AI COGS & margin (02)
5. STRUCTURE  → If a partnership: pick collaboration archetype + term logic (05)
6. GTM        → Land-and-expand motion, pilot design, pipeline (04)
7. VALIDATE   → Stress-test, unit economics, moat, kill criteria (06)
8. PRESENT    → ≥3 options table, recommend one, trade-off visible (Output Contract)
```

## The diagnostic questions (ask/answer before modeling)

1. **Value**: What measurable benefit does the customer realize? In what unit? (₹ saved, units inspected, hours returned, downtime avoided, revenue unlocked)
2. **Attribution**: Can we *prove* our share of that benefit? (determines whether outcome/gainshare is viable vs requires a proxy metric)
3. **Cost shape**: Is our COGS fixed or variable per unit of value? (variable COGS ⇒ never price flat-unlimited)
4. **Budget**: Does the buyer pay from opex or capex? What's their risk appetite? (sets pilot vs deploy, subscription vs purchase)
5. **Moat**: What compounds with each customer/usage? (data, workflow lock-in, integration, exclusive distribution)
6. **Power**: Who owns the customer relationship, the data, and the IP after the deal? (decisive in partnerships — see 05)
7. **Frequency**: One-shot, project, or recurring? (determines recurring-revenue design)

If you cannot answer 1–3, you are not ready to price — go back to value diagnosis.

## Output discipline

Always close with the **≥3-model options table** (mechanism · value metric · why-it-wins · kill-risk · recommend) and **kill criteria**. A single-model answer violates the Iron Law.
